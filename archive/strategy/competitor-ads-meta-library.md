## How to read "winning"

Two signals tell you an ad is working:

1. **Longevity** — a single ad running for months is profitable, so they keep paying. Strongest signal.
2. **Variant volume** — one page running 20–30 near-identical ads = heavy spend + aggressive testing.

Camps: **equipment/hire competitors** (you compete with these) · **event promoters** (sell tickets, not gear) · **adjacent wellness brands** (steal their angles).

## Winning patterns summary

**Hooks that work**
- **Emotion + a specific scene** beats specs. The 6.5-month winner (Silent Disco Stars) opens with "TAG A MATE… 400 people. One dancefloor. Taylor Swift belted at full volume." — zero product talk.
- **Social mechanic** ("TAG A MATE") buys free reach on top of paid.
- **Equipment sellers lead with proof** — volume ("2,000 units"), condition ("like new"), or the 3-channel mechanic.
- **Vertical targeting** works (Louisville → schools; Carter → named cities). Specific beats generic.

**Visual patterns**
- Party/hire winners = **real night-time crowd footage**, glowing LED headphones, balloons/confetti, phone-shot UGC feel — NOT polished studio.
- Equipment sellers = **clean product shots** on white with spec/price overlays.
- Wellness = calm, single-person, soft light (opposite aesthetic).

**Copy structures**
- Party: punchy line breaks, emoji, one vivid image, social CTA.
- Equipment: plain-spoken specs + price + flexibility ("for sale or rent").
- Hire: convenience stack ("free delivery & collection," "packages from 6 to 100+," "any occasion").

**CTAs**
- Hire/rental: **Shop now**, "Book," "packages available."
- Equipment: "for sale or rent" (low-commitment framing).
- Wellness: "Free [thing], every [day]" (lead magnet).

**Your single biggest opening**
Nobody leads with **buy-don't-rent economics for wellness facilitators.** Competitors are either *party hire* (Silent Disco Stars, ssssilentdisco) or *generic/cheap equipment* (Adam Malovani undercutting on price). Your lane — *own it, break even in 3 events, built for breathwork & retreats* — is unclaimed. The play: combine the **emotional-scene hook style of the party winner**, the **proof/spec credibility of the equipment sellers**, and the **wellness hook structure of Tier 3** — pointed at facilitators, not party-throwers.

**One caution on price:** Adam Malovani is publicly advertising $20–30/unit. Don't get dragged into a price war — your wellness positioning, RF reliability (0% failure / 200+ events), and "own forever" math are how you stay premium without competing on his terms.
